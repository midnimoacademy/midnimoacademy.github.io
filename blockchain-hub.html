What to expect

A free, five-lesson beginner course that moves from understanding blockchain records to practising simple smart-contract rules. Each lesson combines English explanations, a short Somali summary, an interactive task and three questions to check your understanding.

Your learning journey

  1. Blocks, hashes and shared records.
  2. Accounts, transactions and network fees.
  3. Smart contracts and access control.
  4. Testing, failures and safe development.
  5. A small application project and next steps.

Time and preparation

Allow around 35–75 minutes per lesson, with extra time for practice. You do not need previous blockchain experience. A laptop is recommended for editing code and using Remix; you can read lessons and use the browser exercises on your phone.

Create a free account and complete your mandatory name, gender, age and location form. Use the same account whenever you return.

What you will practise

Change a block and detect the mismatch, transfer mock units, test who may reset a counter, inspect expected failures and write a short project reflection. You will also receive a Solidity example and guidance for trying it in Remix VM.

How to succeed

  1. Read before clicking. Explain the lesson idea in your own words before starting the task.
  2. Practise both success and failure. Try a permitted action, then an invalid or unauthorised action, and explain the difference.
  3. Complete the task and knowledge check. Finish the practical requirement and answer at least two of the three questions correctly to record a completed lesson.
  4. Learn from feedback. Read each answer explanation, revisit the relevant section and try again when needed.
  5. Keep short notes. Record what you tried, what happened and what still needs explaining. Ask questions using the lesson number and the exact error, without sharing passwords or wallet secrets.
  6. Build a steady routine. Set aside focused study time, take breaks and check your saved progress before leaving.

What this beginner track prepares you for

By the end, you should be able to explain core blockchain concepts, trace a simple transaction, understand the example contract’s permissions and describe a basic testing plan. Depending on your coding experience and further practice, this gives you a foundation for building a small dApp. A production-ready dApp requires additional development, network integration and review.

No cryptocurrency purchase, real wallet or recovery phrase is needed. The browser exercises use teaching models. Course completion records practice; it is not an accredited qualification or a guarantee of employment.
